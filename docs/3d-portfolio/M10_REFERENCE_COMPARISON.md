# SANTRO — Milestone M10: Architectural Reference & Visual Integrity Comparison

## 1. Context & Architectural Mandate

In Milestones M0 through M5, **The Portfolio House** was reconstructed and materially developed directly from the primary architectural video reference (`asset/architectural_reference.mp4`). The visual language is defined by:
- Modernist structural massing and cantilevered volumes.
- Honest architectural materials: architectural concrete, natural stone, dark stained timber, polished terrazzo, and expansive floor-to-ceiling glazing.
- Quiet, contemplative, natural illumination and spatial depth.

Milestone **M10 — Portfolio Content Integration & Readability** introduces the visitor's real engineering work into this house. The paramount aesthetic mandate for M10 is:

> **The house must never look like a commercial trade-show booth, an advertising showroom, or a webpage clumsily mapped onto 3D walls.**

Content must feel like a permanent, curated architectural exhibition within a bespoke residential gallery.

### 2.1 Preserving Architectural Sightlines
In the reference cinematography, the long vistas through the foyer, past the gallery, through the living volume, and out toward the pool and terrace are fundamental to the sense of serenity and scale.
- **M10 Placement Rule**: All primary project steles in the **Project Studio** are positioned along the structural perimeter walls ($x = \pm 2.10\text{ m}$), leaving the central axis clear for the cinematic camera travel path.
- **Glazing Preservation**: No exhibit is placed directly against exterior window panels in a way that blocks exterior natural light or garden reflections.

### 2.2 Materiality of the Exhibition Fixtures
The exhibition fixtures are not generic white 3D boxes. They inherit the physical material palette established in M4:
- **Plinth Bases**: Honed dark volcanic basalt (`#16161A`, roughness `0.78`) and architectural concrete (`#2C2D30`, roughness `0.85`).
- **Display Steles**: Frosted architectural safety glass with subtle chamfered edges, finished with an anti-reflective coating.
- **Hardware Trims**: Anodized brushed architectural aluminum (`#1E1E22`, roughness `0.35`, metalness `0.85`).
- **Workstations**: Integrated timber and steel cabinetry matching the custom joinery observed in the reference interior.

## 3. Lighting Harmony & Absence of "Sci-Fi Glare"

A frequent failure mode in 3D web portfolios is the addition of high-intensity bloom, neon edge glows, and pulsating holographic rings. 

### 3.1 M10 Lighting Discipline
- **Controlled Self-Illumination**: Content surfaces utilize a calibrated low-emissive factor ($0.18 - 0.25$) specifically calibrated to mimic **museum-grade back-illuminated matte panels** (such as e-ink or anti-glare OLED exhibition tablets).
- **Preset Continuity**: When switching between the 5 lighting presets established in M5 (`day`, `golden-hour`, `dusk`, `night`, `studio`), the exhibits respond naturally to the ambient and directional changes while remaining completely legible.
- **Shadow Casting**: Plinth bases cast authentic contact shadows onto terrazzo and timber floors, grounding them firmly in the physical scene.

## 5. Scope Boundary Compliance

Milestone M10 strictly avoids premature features:
- **No M11 Physical Sculptures / Light Art**: Complex bespoke 3D sculptural models for projects remain deferred to M11.
- **No M12 Navigation Overlay Redesign**: Comprehensive mini-maps, full-screen site index drawers, and floating floor plans belong to M12.
- **No M13 Post-Processing Polish**: Bloom passes, lens distortion, and cinematic depth of field belong to M13.

M10 fulfills its contract: **Every project, skill, archive entry, and philosophy pillar is authentically anchored, beautifully legible, and faithfully integrated into the reference architecture.**
